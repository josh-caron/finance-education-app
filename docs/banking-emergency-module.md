# Banking and Emergency Savings

Follows **Money Basics: Budgeting and Saving**. Six lessons and 32 exercises
cover account choice, a starter emergency fund, account fees, and deposit
insurance. Numeric answers are derived with `@fin/core` helpers. Insurance and
fee figures are given inside each problem because those amounts are set by law
or by the institution and can change.

## Lesson sequence

| Lesson                           | Learner outcome                                                                   | Editorial basis                                                          |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Accounts                         | Match account purpose and access to upcoming payments.                            | CFPB toolkit: comparing financial service providers and opening accounts |
| Emergency fund                   | Distinguish unexpected needs, choose a personal target, and calculate deposits.   | CFPB emergency-fund guide and savings booklet                            |
| Fees                             | Calculate stated overdraft and ATM costs and compare fee conditions.              | CFPB toolkit: avoiding checking-account fees                             |
| Insurance                        | Aggregate same-bank deposits and verify bank or credit-union coverage.            | FDIC deposit insurance and NCUA share insurance                          |
| Build a Savings Habit            | Schedule affordable transfers, adapt to income changes, and refill a reserve.     | CFPB emergency-fund guide and cash-flow tools                            |
| Your Banking and Safety-Net Plan | Apply account costs, reserve math, access, and a refill plan to a fictional case. | Synthesis of the same sources                                            |

All exercises include hints and explanations. Insurance ordering uses distinct
uninsured amounts ($0, $50,000, $150,000), so its exact-order grading has no ties.
Overdraft examples explicitly assume opted-in debit-card coverage and the stated
fee; the examples do not imply that every account charges it. Reserve targets are
illustrative choices, not universal CFPB recommendations.

Each unit after the first is locked in the UI until every lesson in the previous
unit is completed: budgeting unlocks banking, banking unlocks money basics.
Existing lesson and exercise IDs are preserved. New lessons must also be completed,
so a learner who finished the former four-lesson unit may see the next unit locked
until the added lessons are finished. Existing attempts and XP are not reset.
